# Decision brief: output template

Deliver the panel's conclusion in this shape. Keep it tight and scannable. A busy founder or consultant should get the call in the first ten seconds and the reasoning if they read on.

---

## Decision
> _One-sentence statement of the real decision, as reframed in step 1._

**Options considered:** _A · B · C (and "do nothing" if relevant)._
**Stakes / reversibility:** _one line on how consequential it is and how hard to undo._

## The recommendation
**→ [Chosen option].** Confidence: **[High / Medium / Low]**

_The 2–3 decisive reasons, in order of weight. Rank the alternatives: [A] over [B] over [C], and one line on why each falls where it does._

## Did the panel beat one good answer?

**Solo baseline (taken before the panel convened):** _[recommendation] · confidence [H/M/L] · [the single reason that drove it]._

**Verdict: [Panel changed the call / Panel changed the confidence / Panel added a decisive risk / Panel added nothing].**

_One or two lines: exactly what the panel added, quoting the take or review line it came from (the gates in `verdict-rubric.md` decide the verdict). If it added nothing, say so plainly: "The panel did not beat the single answer here. You could have asked once." Then one line on what kind of decision **would** have needed a panel._

**Which round did the work:** _the independent takes, or the blind peer review, or neither, said plainly._

**The gate:** _the three scouts split [how], which is why a panel was convened. (Note any scout whose full take differed from its one-line probe.)_

> _This section is mandatory. A brief without it is incomplete. Decision Panels reports this on every run, including the runs where the answer is unflattering. That is what makes the other runs worth believing._

## The panel
_One line per panelist: name, position, and the crux of its reasoning. Positions are the ones held **after** the blind review; note any that moved._
- **[Lens]:** [position], [why]
- **[Lens]:** [position], [why] _(moved after review: [the argument that moved it])_
- _(…5–7 lenses)_

## What the blind review caught
_The panelists read each other's answers anonymised, without knowing who wrote what. Keep this to three lines._

- **What everyone missed:** _the consideration absent from every independent take, usually the most valuable line in the brief._
- **Biggest blind spot named:** _which position had it, and what it was._
- **Who moved, and who held:** _positions that changed and the specific argument that changed them; anyone who held out under pressure and why they were not answered. If a position moved with no new argument behind it, say so; that is conformity, not agreement._

_If the review round changed nothing, say that in one line rather than dressing it up._

## The real disagreement
_The sharpest genuine split **that survived cross-examination**, both sides steelmanned. This is the part a single opinion would have hidden._

## The strongest case against the recommendation (preserved dissent)
_Stated so a smart skeptic would nod. Do not soften it. If the recommendation is wrong, this is probably why._

## What would change the call
_The specific new information or changed condition that would flip the ranking, i.e. what to go find out before (or instead of) committing._

# The short brief, for when the gate stopped the run

Use this shape when step 4 converged and no panel was convened. It is a complete deliverable, not a stub: the user still gets a call they can act on, and an honest account of why it cost them less than they expected.

---

## Decision
> _One-sentence statement of the real decision, as reframed in step 1._

**Options considered:** _A · B · C (and "do nothing" if relevant)._
**Stakes / reversibility:** _one line, noting that this is why gating was allowed at all._

## The recommendation
**→ [Chosen option].** Confidence: **[High / Medium / Low]**

_The 2–3 decisive reasons, in order of weight._

## No panel was convened, and here's why
_Three lenses that disagree about most things were asked for a one-line read: **[Lens A]**, **[Lens B]**, **[Lens C]**. [Two/Three] of them picked the same option **and** named the same crux._

**The crux they all landed on:** _the fact or assumption this decision actually turns on._

> _"A panel was unlikely to change this call, so I didn't run one."_

**What would have made me convene:** _the specific thing that would have split them, so the user learns when a panel is worth asking for._

## What would change the call
_The specific new information or changed condition that would flip it._

---

**Want the full panel anyway?** _Say so and it runs: 5–7 lenses, independent takes, blind peer review, the disagreement preserved. The gate is a default, not a refusal._
