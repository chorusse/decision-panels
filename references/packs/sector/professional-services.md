# Sector pack: owner-operated professional services

**Tier:** sector. Composes on top of `general/common-lenses.md` and underneath a vertical pack. See
SKILL.md step 0.

**Load this pack when:** the business sells the judgement of licensed or credentialed people, bills
for it by time or by engagement, and the owner is also a senior producer. Accounting and tax
practices, law firms, advisory and consulting practices, registered investment advisers, agencies
whose product is expert opinion.

**Do not load it when:** the business sells a product, a platform, or labour whose value is not the
practitioner's individual judgement. A trades business with licensed staff is not this shape; the
distinguishing feature is that clients are buying *who* does the work.

**What this tier is for:** the tensions common to every practice of this shape, so no vertical pack
has to restate them, and so the claims stay honest about being general rather than disguised as
one profession's domain knowledge.

## 1. Decision classes covered
- **Client portfolio:** "should I fire this client, or stop taking this kind of work?"
- **Billing model:** "do I move off the hourly bill, and to what?"
- **Work mix:** "do I stay with recurring commodity work or move toward judgement work?"
- **Leverage:** "do I do this myself, or build people who can?"
- **Succession and exit:** "do I sell, hand it to staff, wind down, or keep going?"

## 2. Fault lines: where practices genuinely split

- **Recurring work ⟷ judgement work:** recurring compliance-type work is predictable, referable and
  renews itself ⟷ it is also the most exposed to price pressure and automation, and every year spent
  defending it is a year not spent building the practice that isn't.
- **Realisation ⟷ relationship:** the client who pays late, scope-creeps and destroys your
  realisation rate is a rational fire ⟷ that same client is often several referrals deep into your
  best segment, and the loss never appears on their own line.
- **Owner as producer ⟷ owner as principal:** the owner doing the senior work is usually why quality
  holds and margin exists ⟷ it is also the reason the practice depends on one person's availability,
  and the work of building anyone else competes directly with billable hours.
  > ⚠️ **Contested, do not assert.** The stronger version of this claim, that an owner-producer
  > practice therefore *has no sale value*, is **plausible and unvalidated**. Practices with a
  > working owner do sell. What a buyer pays for, and how much client transferability actually
  > discounts it, varies by profession, buyer type and market. A lens may raise transferability as a
  > **question**; no lens may tell the user their practice is unsaleable.
- **Client concentration ⟷ growth:** the few large clients that make the numbers work are also the
  ones whose departure would be existential, and the effort to diversify is effort taken from
  serving them well.

## 3. Lens roster
- **The Realisation Lens.** *Remit:* what the work actually collects per hour after write-offs and
  scope creep, not what it was quoted at. *Argues for:* repricing or releasing the bottom of the
  book. *Bias:* treats every relationship as substitutable. *Crux:* is the write-off concentrated in
  a few fixable clients, or spread across the whole segment?
- **The Referral-Web Lens.** *Remit:* the client as a node, not a line item; who came from whom.
  *Argues for:* keeping clients whose value is off their own invoice. *Bias:* can justify keeping
  anyone. *Crux:* can you name the last three referrals this client actually produced?
- **The Commoditisation Lens.** *Remit:* which parts of the book are being automated or priced out
  from under the practice, and how fast. *Argues for:* moving toward judgement work before the price
  moves. *Bias:* perpetually early; has been forecasting the death of routine work for a long time.
  *Crux:* is the client paying for the deliverable, or for someone to be responsible for it?
- **The Practice-Value Lens.** *Remit:* what the practice is worth to a buyer or a successor, and
  whether this decision moves that. *Argues for:* transferable clients, documented process, reduced
  owner dependence. *Bias:* optimises for a sale the owner may not want, and treats transferability
  as an unqualified good. *Crux:* is the owner actually exiting within ten years, or is this
  hypothetical?

## 4. Scout triples
- **Client portfolio:** Realisation · Referral-Web · Customer-Outcome. *Convene if:* they split on
  whether the client's value is on or off the invoice.
- **Billing model:** Realisation · Commoditisation · Customer-Outcome. *Convene if:* they disagree
  about whether the client is buying the deliverable or the responsibility.
- **Work mix:** Commoditisation · Realisation · Capacity Realist. *Convene if:* they split on
  whether the constraint is demand for judgement work or the ability to deliver it.
- **Leverage:** Capacity Realist · Practice-Value · Staff. *Convene if:* they disagree about whether
  the owner is the asset or the bottleneck.
- **Succession and exit:** Practice-Value · Staff · Customer-Outcome. *Convene if:* they disagree
  about who the practice is actually for.

## 5. Reversibility map: one-way doors
- **One-way: firing a client segment.** *Why it doesn't undo:* they place elsewhere quickly and the
  referral web reroutes around you; you do not get the segment back by changing your mind later.
- **One-way: selling the practice.** *Why it doesn't undo:* self-evident, and an earn-out means the
  owner lives inside the consequence for years.
- **One-way (soft): moving a long-standing client from hourly to fixed fee.** *Why it doesn't undo:*
  going back reads as a price rise plus an admission the first change was wrong.
- **Two-way (gate freely):** one client's engagement letter, a single season's outsourcing
  arrangement, a subcontracting relationship, taking on one client of a new type as a trial.

## 6. Calibration questions
- Is the practice something you intend to leave one day, by sale, handover or winding down, or is
  that not in view? *Changes:* whether Practice-Value is seated at all, and whether transferability
  counts as a cost.
- What proportion of revenue comes from your five largest clients? *Changes:* whether this is a
  portfolio decision or a concentration-risk decision.
- Are you the only person who can do the senior work, and is that by circumstance or by choice?
  *Changes:* whether leverage is a live option or a fantasy.

## 7. Domain guardrails: what no lens may assert
- **Never state professional-conduct rules, licensure requirements, independence obligations or
  scope-of-practice limits as fact.** Frame each as "confirm with your professional body or state
  board," and treat the answer as an input the panel does not have.
- **Never quote practice-valuation multiples.** They vary by profession, buyer and market, and a
  confident number here is the single most damaging thing this pack could produce.
- **Never assert that owner involvement destroys practice value.** See the contested flag in §2. It
  is a question to raise, never a finding to report.
- **Never assume the practice is a partnership, or that it isn't.** Ownership structure changes who
  the decision is even for.

## 8. What "the panel beat the baseline" usually looks like here
- *Most common honest verdict:* **Panel added a decisive risk**, because the single answer is
  usually right on the arithmetic and misses the second-order consequence in the referral web, the
  seniority ladder, or the concentration profile.
- *The flattery trap:* manufacturing a split between Realisation and Referral-Web on a client who is
  plainly unprofitable *and* refers nobody. When the answer is obvious the gate should stop the run.

## 9. Anti-patterns
- **Do not seat Realisation and Capacity Realist as a pair without an opposing lens.** They agree by
  construction on almost every capacity question.
- **Do not seat Commoditisation, Practice-Value and Customer-Outcome together on a billing call.**
  They converge on "move up-market" from three directions and the panel becomes a chorus.
- **The default false consensus in this sector is "fire the bottom 20% of the book and raise
  prices."** It is often right, which is exactly why Referral-Web and Staff need seating to break
  it; the cost of being wrong lands where the P&L doesn't show it for a year.
