# Panel lenses

A library of advisor lenses to seat on a decision panel. Pick **5–7 that fit the specific decision**, never the same default set every time. Each lens argues its worldview honestly, including where that puts it in conflict with the others. Craft bespoke lenses when the decision needs expertise not covered here (e.g. a regulatory lens for a compliance call).

Aim for a panel that will *genuinely disagree*. A good panel pairs at least one lens that wants to act with one that wants to wait, and at least one that speaks for someone not in the room.

## Core lenses (general decisions)

- **The Operator**: what actually ships and works with the resources on hand. Cheapest, fastest viable path; allergic to plans that require everything to go right. Bias: underweights the big upside.
- **The Skeptic**: attacks the appealing option, names the failure modes, asks "what has to be true for this to work?" Surfaces the strongest counterargument. Bias: can talk you out of good bold moves.
- **The Long-Game Strategist**: second- and third-order effects, five years out; what compounds, what becomes a trap. Bias: discounts urgent near-term costs.
- **The Numbers**: unit economics, expected value, what the math says regardless of the story. Forces quantification of hand-wavy claims. Bias: undervalues what's hard to measure.
- **The Customer / End-User Advocate**: what the person on the receiving end actually wants, understands, and will do. Bias: can privilege the user over the business's survival.
- **The Visionary**: the ambitious, ideal-state answer that ignores current constraints to find the best *possible* move, then names the one constraint that really matters. Bias: over-optimistic on execution.
- **The Steward of Resources**: time, cash, attention, and morale as scarce budgets; opportunity cost of every yes. Bias: can starve worthwhile bets.
- **The Reversibility Lens**: how hard is this to undo? Argues for cheap tests before irreversible commitments; distinguishes one-way from two-way doors. Bias: can over-delay genuinely urgent one-way calls.

## Founder / startup decisions
- **The Runway Realist**: what this does to cash and time-to-default; can you afford to be wrong?
- **The Focus Hawk**: every yes is a no to something else; is this the *one* thing?
- **The Investor**: how this reads to the next round; narrative, milestones, and signaling.
- **The Team Lens**: effect on the people who have to execute it; hiring, burnout, trust.

## Product / PM decisions
- **The User-Value Lens**: does this make the core job meaningfully better, or is it motion?
- **The Data Lens**: what usage/experiments actually say vs. what we wish they said.
- **The Craft Lens**: quality, coherence, and what shipping this does to the product's soul.
- **The Cost-of-Delay Lens**: what waiting costs vs. what rushing breaks.

## Consultant / advisory decisions
- **The Client-Outcome Lens**: what genuinely serves the client, even if it's not what they asked for.
- **The Relationship Lens**: trust, scope, and the long arc of the engagement.
- **The Defensibility Lens**: can you stand behind this recommendation when it's questioned?

## Personal / high-stakes life decisions
- **The Values Lens**: which option is most in line with who you want to be, independent of outcome.
- **The Future-Self Lens**: which will you be glad you chose in ten years; regret-minimization.
- **The Honest-Friend Lens**: what someone who loves you *and* will tell you the truth would say.

## Composing a panel: quick guidance
- Always seat at least one lens that will argue *against* the option the user seems to favor.
- Include a lens that speaks for a stakeholder who isn't in the conversation.
- For irreversible or bet-the-company calls, always seat the **Skeptic** and the **Reversibility Lens**.
- 5 lenses for most calls; 7 when the decision is genuinely complex or contested. More than 7 dilutes rather than sharpens.
